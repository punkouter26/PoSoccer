# ML-Agents audit — 2026-09-07

Scope: training configs, reward shaping, observation/action spaces, agent logic,
bio-realistic locomotion, training throughput. Ten items were requested; what follows
is what each one turned out to be once measured, including the four that were already
done and the one whose premise did not survive contact with the hardware.

Every number here is measured or computed from a file in the repo. Where a claim in
CLAUDE.md turned out to be stale, that is called out rather than quietly corrected.

## Headline: two things were true and unrecorded

**1. p22 finished, reached the full-strength bot, and was never graded.**
`results/soccer_p22gain_standard/run_logs/training_status.json` shows 10,000,986 steps
and `bot_strength: lesson_num 5` — **Lesson5_Full, bot_strength 1.0**. That is the first
policy in this project's history to train against the same opponent `evaluate.ps1`
grades on, which had been the blocking hypothesis since p17. Its `.onnx` was deployed
to `Standard_v01` at 03:14 and `evalWinRate` was still `-1`, with no `results/eval/`
directory at all.

### The p22 grade, and what it rules out

**26.6% (93/350), red 46.0%, stalemates 27.4%.** Bar is ≥80% wins / ≤10% stalemates.

The grade is sound, and the provenance in the JSON is what proves it: `modelPath` is
`Assets\Agents\Standard_v01\STANDARD.onnx`, `modelWrittenUtc` (07:14 UTC) precedes
`playerBuiltUtc` (18:08 UTC) so the player is not stale, and `modelInputs` sums to
39+15+39+27+58 = **178**, exactly the declared contract.

| | p21 | p22 |
|---|---|---|
| ActionGain | 1.6 | **1.0** |
| curriculum reached | Lesson3_Capable (0.65) | **Lesson5_Full (1.0)** |
| blue wins (n=350) | 25.7% | **26.6%** |
| red wins | 49.7% | 46.0% |
| stalemates | 24.6% | 27.4% |

**+0.9 pp against a 3.3 pp SD of the difference — 0.26 SD. Noise**, by this project's own
"never read a <10-point gap as signal" rule.

**Two hypotheses die here, and the second one matters.**

1. The `ActionGain` 1.6 → 1.0 fix did not move the win rate. It remains justified on the
   dead-band argument and should be kept, but it is not the lever.
2. **Training against the full-strength bot did not move the win rate either.** This was
   *the* blocking hypothesis since p17 — "no policy in this project has ever trained
   against a competent opponent", the stated reason two large locomotion wins converted
   losses into draws instead of wins. p22 climbed the entire ladder to `bot_strength 1.0`,
   the exact opponent eval grades on, and still graded 26.6%. The train/eval opponent
   mismatch is now **closed as an explanation**.

That is what makes the reward-budget finding below the leading candidate rather than one
item on a list: with the curriculum explanation spent, a table in which four dense terms
can outrank the terminal rewards is the most direct account left of why a policy that
locomotes well and trains against the right opponent still does not score.

**3. Three of the four shipped brains were silently out-of-distribution.**
`Agent_Soccer.ActionGain` is 1.0 (changed for p22). MATT, NICK and KIM are all
`soccer_p21_*` checkpoints trained at **1.6**. Tensor shapes are identical, so they load
without a single warning and then under-drive.

Worth keeping the severity straight: this is **degraded, not scrambled**. A gain change
is a monotonic scaling — direction preserved, magnitude lost — unlike the 2026-08-28
world→body frame change, which altered what an observation *meant*. Both are silent,
which is why both need a guard rather than a paragraph.

## The substantive finding: the dense reward table could outrank its own objective

Nobody had computed what a dense term accumulates over a whole episode. They are written
as small numbers and read as negligible beside a 1.2 goal. They are charged **every
physics step** (`OnActionReceived` runs at 100 Hz on the 0.01 s timestep; ML-Agents
repeats the last action between decisions) against a **9000-step cap**:

| term | old value | per-episode ceiling | vs terminal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ballToGoalVelocityScale` | 0.001 | **+9.0** | 7.5× a goal (+1.2) |
| `cornerBallPenalty` | 0.0006 | **−5.4** | 5.4× conceding (−1.0) |
| `wallProximityPenalty` | 0.0005 | **−4.5** | 4.5× conceding |
| `crossbarProximity` | 0.0005 | **+4.5** | 3.75× a goal |
| `possessionScale` (NICK) | 0.0006 | **+5.4** | 4.5× a goal |
| `defensivePositionScale` (KIM) | 0.0006 | **+5.4** | 4.2× KIM's goal (1.3) |
| `stepPenalty` | −0.00005 | −0.45 | ok |
| `facingAlignmentScale` | 0.00005 | +0.45 | ok, but redundant |
| `actionJitterScale` | −0.0004 | −0.45 | ok (per-decision) |

A policy maximising return under that table should farm ball-goal velocity and avoid
corners, **not** score and defend. NICK was paid up to 4.5× more for holding the ball
than for scoring with it.

These are *maxima*, requiring the condition to hold every step; real duty cycles are
lower. The defect is that the ceiling is reachable at all — it means the table does not
guarantee the ordering of outcomes.

**The arithmetic is validated against an independent figure.** CLAUDE.md derives
`stepPenalty`'s real cost from eval telemetry as `5784 × 0.00005 = 0.289`. The same model
reproduces 0.289 for that term at that episode length.

**The method was already in the codebase.** `stepPenalty`'s own comment does exactly this
budget arithmetic ("the episode cap is 9000 steps, so the old −0.0001 cost −0.9 per
episode — near-identical to `goalConceded` (−1.0) — and drowned every other term"). It had
simply never been applied to the other seven terms.

This is a more direct account of the signature "learns not to lose, never learns to win"
plateau than the curriculum story it was attributed to. It does **not** retract the p21
curriculum result (25.7%, ~4 SD over the plateau) — both can be real, and p21 climbed the
ladder with this table still in place.

### What changed

Every per-step term divided by 10, preserving trait *ratios* between personalities so the
characters stay recognisable; `facingAlignmentScale` and `crossbarProximity` retired
outright as redundant — bearing to the ball has been an **observation** since the
body-frame fix, and a ball in the goal mouth moving netward is already paid by
`ballToGoalVelocityScale` and then by `goalScorer`.

Code defaults and all five assets moved together, because
`RewardProfiles_MatchCodeDefaultsOnMechanics` pins them to each other and because a
changed initializer never reaches an existing asset.

Post-change ceilings, all profiles passing:

| profile | largest terminal | largest dense ceiling | positive total (limit 3×goal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| STANDARD | 1.20 | 0.90 | 0.90 |
| MATT | 1.40 | 0.99 | 0.99 |
| KIM | 1.30 | 0.54 | 0.54 |
| NICK | 1.20 | 0.90 | 0.90 |
| BOT | 1.20 | 0.90 | 0.90 |

Pinned by the new `Agent_EditMode_RewardBudget`, which asserts the **ordering property**
(no single dense term may outweigh the largest terminal reward) rather than any specific
table, and fails if a future `maxEnvironmentSteps` increase re-breaks it — the ceiling is
linear in the step cap.

## Sensor geometry (measured, not changed)

Pitch is 36 × 54 (half extents 18 × 27, read live from `SCN_Training`), agent collider
0.8, spherecast radius 0.1, max separation 64.9 u.

| sensor | rays | distinct dirs | spacing | length | guaranteed detect | obs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ball | 13 | 12 | 30° | 24 | ≤ 1.93 u | 39 |
| Goal | 5 | 5 | 15° | 40 | ≤ 3.83 u | 15 |
| Opponents | 13 | 12 | 30° | 24 | ≤ 1.93 u | 39 |
| Walls | 9 | 8 | 45° | 12 | ≤ 1.31 u | 27 |

The 1.93 u figure reproduces CLAUDE.md's independently-stated value, which validates the
geometry model.

Three range mismatches, all against the pitch the sensors actually run on:

1. **Wall rays are 12 u; the nearest wall from the pitch centre is 18 u.** There is a
   blind rectangle |x| < 6, |y| < 15 = 360 of 1944 sq units — **18.5% of the pitch** where
   no wall ray can return a hit. `Sensor_Vision`'s own comment says walls sit "max 30 m
   from centre" and then sets the length to 12.
2. **Goal rays are 40 u; the far goal is up to 54 u away.** An agent on its own goal line
   cannot ray-see the opponent's.
3. **Ball rays are 24 u on a 54 u pitch** — a ball in the far third is out of range.

**Why this is reported and not fixed.** The vector observation already carries `relBall`,
both goal positions and both opponent slots, all unbounded and body-frame, so the rays are
largely redundant *for position*; their unique contribution is occlusion. And an arc or
range change leaves every tensor shape identical while the rays report a different world —
the documented landmine that cost phases 9/10. Any change here is a full retrain and needs
its own single-variable run, not a drive-by edit.

The cost is real though: **120 of 178 model inputs (67.4%) are rays**, and the two cheapest
to justify dropping (Goal, 15 obs; Walls, 27 obs) are the two most redundant with the
vector obs. That is the experiment to run, deliberately, after the reward work settles.

## Throughput

**Baseline: 642.2 s per million steps**, computed from p22's own checkpoint timestamps
across nine 1M-step intervals (637–658, very stable). This is a good control for an A/B.

**The multithreading premise did not survive the hardware check.** The proposal was to set
`Physics2DSettings.useMultithreading: 1`. This machine is an **i7-10750H: 6 physical cores,
12 logical**, and a training run is already **4 env processes + 1 trainer**. Per-process
physics worker jobs would contend for cores that are already saturated, and with
`useConsistencySorting: 0` it also makes contact resolution non-deterministic. Not flipped.

The levers that would actually pay — `m_VelocityIterations: 8` / `m_PositionIterations: 3`
down to 6/2, and `m_QueriesStartInColliders: 1` (every ray currently starts inside the
agent's own collider) — all change contact dynamics, so they need a pilot rather than an
assertion. Left as a measured proposal.

**`num_envs` corrected in 18 configs.** Every `TRAIN_*` and `STANDARD_phase*` config
declared `num_envs: 1` while `train-phase1.ps1` passes `--num-envs=4` on the command line.
The CLI wins, so nothing was broken — but the `configuration.yaml` archived with each run
disagreed with the run that produced it (p22's record reads 4 against a config file
reading 1), and a run launched without the script silently drops to a quarter throughput
while looking identical in the archive.

## Four items that were already done

Reported because the audit's value is partly in what it *cleared*, and because two of
these premises came from stale CLAUDE.md text that should not be trusted again.

- **Stripping presentation from the agent.** Already done. Cosmetics live in
  `Agent_SoccerView.Build` and are already gated behind
  `!EvalMode && !Academy.IsCommunicatorOn`, so a headless run builds no LineRenderers and
  no TextMesh. The 945-line file is **514 code / 336 comment (36%)**, and those comments
  record retractions — documentation, not bloat.
- **Stamina recovery.** Already implemented, and well: delayed onset (0.6 s),
  fullness-ramped rate, a `depletedRecoveryFactor` so recovery from empty is slower than
  topping up, and a partial-tick credit fix. CLAUDE.md's "no recovery path" line is
  **stale** — it describes cross-match *wear* recovery, which is a game-side design
  decision documented in `rules-exemptions.md` §4, not a training defect. Wear also
  already resets per episode on headless runs, closing the non-stationarity hole.
- **Bio-realistic locomotion.** Already thorough within the single-rigidbody constraint:
  one friction circle of `mu·m·g` shared by launching/cutting/braking, active foot braking
  out of spare traction, anisotropic drag so strafing is slower than running, turn
  authority falling from 360°/s at rest to 25% at sprint, force slew, mass-scaled drive,
  and contact-based drive authority.
- **Ragdoll joint limits.** There are none to tune — there is no articulated body. Each
  player is a single `Rigidbody2D` (`Agent_Contact.cs:9`), recorded as an **open
  deviation** in `rules-exemptions.md` §2, deferred because per-joint state and drive
  targets would change both the observation and action spaces and obsolete every `.onnx`.

## Jitter is charged per decision, not per step

`actionJitterScale` compares against `_prevActions`, which is rewritten every physics
step — but between decisions the action buffer is *identical*, so the term evaluates to
zero on 7 steps out of 8 and only bites on the decision step. The behaviour is correct;
the hazard is that anyone tuning it against `ballProximityScale` would be off by 8×. The
budget test models it explicitly at `cap / DecisionPeriod` so the asymmetry is written
down rather than rediscovered.

## RETRACTION — p23 was killed on a signal that did not exist

p23 (the capped table) was stopped at 600k because its mean reward sat flat between
−1.11 and −1.23 across six consecutive summaries. I read that as a starved gradient and
switched to the potential-based design. **The premise was wrong, and it was checkable
before I acted on it.**

Pulling every run's curve out of TensorBoard and putting them on the same axis:

| step | p22 (old table, graded **26.6%**) | p23 (capped) | p24 (potential) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100k | −1.387 | −1.228 | −1.602 |
| 300k | −1.392 | −1.206 | −1.184 |
| 500k | −1.222 | −1.209 | — |
| 700k | −1.220 | −1.201 | — |
| 1.0M | −1.033 | — | — |
| 2.0M | −0.81 | — | — |
| 3.0M | **+0.16** | — | — |

**p22 sat at −1.2 through its first 700k as well**, and it is the best run this project
has produced. The flat stretch at −1.2 is not a starved gradient — it is what every run
here looks like before ~800k. p23 was behaving normally and was killed for it.

Two things follow, and only one of them is comfortable:

1. **The p24 design change is still independently justified.** The old ball→goal term
   was a rate: unbounded, and farmable by knocking the ball goalward and letting it roll
   back. Potential-based shaping fixes that on its own merits and would have been worth
   testing regardless. That argument never depended on p23's curve.
2. **The decision procedure was still wrong.** I had a cheap, decisive measurement
   available — p22's own curve, already on disk — and I inferred from a pattern instead
   of reading it. This is the same failure the phase 6–16 arc is made of: a plausible
   mechanism plus a real-looking symptom is not a diagnosis. The cost here was small
   (~600k steps, ~7 minutes) only because the run was short.

**Operational rule this establishes: on this task, no run is interpretable before ~1M
steps, and the discriminating window is 2.5–3M.** p22 was still at −1.03 at 1M and only
crossed zero at 3M. Any judgement of a 3M pilot made before ~2M is reading noise. Put the
candidate curve next to p22's on the same axis before concluding anything — the data is
in `results/<run>/STANDARD/events.out.tfevents.*` and takes seconds to extract.

## RESULT — imitation made it WORSE (p26, graded)

| run | lever | graded win rate (n=350) | red | stalemates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `soccer_p22gain_standard` | ActionGain 1.0 + full curriculum | **26.6%** | 46.0% | 27.4% |
| `soccer_p26imitate_standard` | BC (500k) + GAIL 0.3 | **12.6%** | 53.7% | 33.7% |

−14.0 pp on a combined SD of ~3.0 pp — roughly **4.7 SD**. This is not noise and not a
wash: behavioural cloning plus GAIL, as configured, **halved** the win rate.

The grade is sound (`modelWrittenUtc` 20:13:37 precedes `playerBuiltUtc` 20:14:32,
model inputs sum to 178).

**And the training curve pointed the other way.** Over 1.8–2.3M p26 averaged −0.324
against p24's −0.700, a large and consistent lead at every matched step — I read that as
imitation working. By the 2.6–3.0M window it had inverted (p26 −0.418, p24 −0.278), and
the eval says worse still. That is the **third** time in this session a mid-training
curve pointed the opposite way from the graded outcome.

**The operational rule, restated harder: on this task, mean reward is not a decision
variable.** Adjacent summaries swing by ±0.7, which is wider than any effect worth
detecting. Only the graded win rate decides. I wrote that rule down after the p23
retraction and then failed to apply it twice more.

### Why imitation plausibly backfired (hypotheses, not measured)

1. **Covariate shift** — the textbook BC failure. Demos are bot-vs-bot; the learner
   faces bot-vs-itself *while losing*, drifting into states the demonstrator never
   visited and where it has no recovery behaviour. DAgger exists for exactly this.
2. **GAIL competes with winning.** At strength 0.3 it rewards *resembling* the
   demonstrator's state distribution — a distribution the agent cannot reach — against
   an extrinsic signal that rewards scoring. The stalemate rise (27.4% → 33.7%) is
   consistent with a policy optimising to look busy rather than to finish.
3. **BC anchoring.** 500k steps of supervised pull may leave a policy RL then has to
   spend its budget undoing.

Distinguishing these needs an ablation (BC alone, GAIL alone, on-policy demos), not
another combined run.

### Scoreboard after this session

Five levers, all measured, none positive:

| lever | outcome |
|---|---|
| curriculum saturation (p22, full-strength bot) | +0.9 pp — noise |
| reward budget caps (p23) | abandoned; retracted as premature |
| potential-based shaping (p24) | +0.049 vs p22 at matched steps — wash |
| imitation BC+GAIL (p26) | **−14.0 pp — significantly worse** |
| episode-length lever (p25) | built and guarded, **never run** |

The one lever in this project's history that ever moved the graded number remains p21's
curriculum (+9.1 pp). Everything since has been flat or negative.

**What this argues for next.** Not another 3M pilot. Either (a) run p25 — the only
untried mechanism, and the one with an argued mechanism behind it (~3500x more terminal
signal, ~3.6x more terminal events per million steps), or (b) accept that 3M pilots
cannot resolve differences on this task and spend the compute on one long run instead.
The 80% bar needs a structural change; five reward/initialisation levers have now failed
to move it.
